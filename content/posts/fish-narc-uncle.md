In the 2010s, I was a big Lil Peep fan, and through his music I found out about his associates in GBC, namely fish narc, Horse Head, Wicca Phase Springs Eternal, and Lil Tracy. They were known for quirky trap production that sampled grunge and emo music, as well as bleak, nihilistic lyrics about girls, drugs, violence, and so on. I mostly associated fish narc with his production work from that time, so I was a little surprised to come across *Uncle* and find out that his solo project is apparently a band making '90s-style college rock now.

I've been missing this kind of easy-listening radio-station rock lately. There's something really refreshing about an album that's this straightforward and has such a clearly defined sound. It's a brief 26 minutes long, with a tracklist full of songs that are both pleasing to the ears and very, very familiar.

What surprised me most was how well put together it all is. The mix sounds just as blown-out and over the top as it needs to, with heavy distorted guitars, simple drum grooves, and fish narc's disaffected, low-key moaning sharing the space pretty elegantly. You won't find any groundbreaking experimental instrumentation or crazy vocal performances here, though there are some great solos. Some of the melodies and arrangements even reminded me a little bit of New Radicals, or maybe Blur or The Verve, for some reason. I also like the acoustic guitar-driven closer.

The lyrics are nothing exceptional, but they have some pleasing wordplay and a good flow to them. They touch on the usual themes of love, addiction, coming of age, and financial problems. The standout for me is the girlfriend-worship anthem "Emma," which has some crazy, religious-cult levels of adoration going on.

Overall, good album. If you're in the mood for some really competent, easy-listening rock, give it a spin on [Spotify](https://open.spotify.com/album/1XQb7RxEYgdKlJu69rctRA?si=OFE7gN_DRuqgtpKdXsDsWw).
